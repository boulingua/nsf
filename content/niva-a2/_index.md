## What changes at A2

Three things, and they are worth naming because learners usually notice only
the first.

**Tense.** Norwegian past-tense morphology is regular enough to teach as a
system and irregular enough to need a list. The course teaches *preteritum*
and *perfektum* as two separate units rather than one, because German-speaking
learners arrive with a strong *Perfekt* habit and need to be shown that the
Norwegian division of labour is different: *Jeg var i Bergen i fjor*, not
*Jeg har vært i Bergen i fjor*.

**Subordination.** The V2 rule learned at A1 stops applying inside a
subordinate clause, and the negation moves in front of the verb: *Jeg tror at
hun **ikke kommer***. This is the single most productive structural step of
the level and gets a unit of its own.

**Register, in outline.** Not the full B1 treatment, but the beginning of it:
*du* to everyone, but not quite everything; how a text message differs from a
note to a teacher.

## The Scandinavian thread at A2

Unit 12 is the level's receptive-Scandinavian unit and goes considerably
further than its A1 predecessor. Learners read a genuine Danish text and a
genuine Swedish text — a recipe, a museum notice, a short news item — with
Norwegian as the working language throughout, and build the two habits that
actually transfer: reading *through* an unfamiliar spelling to a familiar
word (Norwegian *sj-* against Swedish *sk-*, *ø* against *ö*, *æ* against
*ä*, Danish *-b-/-d-/-g-* where Norwegian writes *-p-/-t-/-k-*), and
tolerating the
handful of *falske venner* that trip Scandinavians up among themselves.

The claim stays scoped. A learner finishing A2 should be able to get the sense
of written Danish and Swedish and to follow slow, clear Swedish speech. Danish
speech is harder for Norwegians themselves, and the course says so instead of
promising it.

## Culture, without the postcard

Units 9 and 10 carry the level's cultural weight and try to do it without
turning Norway into a tourist brochure. *17. mai* is taught as what it is — a
children's parade, a constitution, a set of clothes with regional meaning, and
a school-leavers' fortnight that Norwegians themselves argue about.
*Friluftsliv* is taught alongside *allemannsretten* and the practical
business of being cold, which is what the concept mostly consists of.

## The model exam

**A2 — Modellprøve**, a sibling bundle of Unit 12 with the same `unit_nr`,
published as an HTML page with a committed PDF under
`static/downloads/a2/`. Four parts — *lytting*, *lesing*, *skriving*,
*muntlig* — 100 points, pass mark 60 %. The writing task is a short
connected text rather than a form: at A2 the assessable skill is
subordination and tense, and a form does not show either.

## Curriculum alignment

Units carry `curriculum.framework: cefr`, `cefr_level: A2` and `cefr_can_do`
statements referencing `A2.{DOMAIN}.{SCALE}.{SEQ}` descriptor IDs. A2 is the
level at which the Companion Volume's mediation scales first offer usable
descriptors for a foreign-language classroom, and Unit 12 is written against
them rather than around them.
