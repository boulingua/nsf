A Norwegian course for people who will never live inside the language full-time
and still want to read it, hear it and use it well. The written variety is
**Bokmål**; the spoken norm behind the audio is **Eastern (Oslo) Bokmål**. The
course runs from a short pronunciation stage through the CEFR levels **A1, A2
and B1**, each closing with a model examination. Everything is openly licensed
and free to reuse.
{{< /lead >}}
{{< /hero >}}

## What this is

A curriculum-aligned Norwegian course built to the shared boulingua standard:
page-per-unit articles, a model exam as a first-class sibling of the last unit
in each level, downloadable open-format materials (editable `.odp` decks and
printable PDF worksheets), and native-voice audio with a written transcript
beside every recording.

Every unit follows the same five moves — **Activate → Input → Practise → Apply
→ Reflect** — so that learners can predict the shape of a lesson and teachers
can drop differentiated material into any step without redesigning the unit.

## Who it is for

- **Pupils** at a German *Gesamtschule* taking Norwegian as a third or further
  foreign language, and the teachers who have to prepare those lessons.
- **Independent adult learners** who want a structured route to B1 rather than
  a phrasebook.
- **Anyone reusing the material** under an open licence — adapt it, translate
  the glosses, print the worksheets.

The classroom metalanguage is German, with English glosses where they carry
more weight than a German equivalent; the target language is Norwegian
throughout, from the first unit onwards.

## Scope, stated honestly

The declared conformance target is **`core` — A1 to B1**. Every in-scope CEFR
scale that carries an official descriptor at A1, A2 or B1 is implemented;
where the Companion Volume leaves a cell empty, the course records
`no-official-descriptor` rather than inventing one. **B2 and C1 are out of the
current target** and are not pretended otherwise. Openly-licensed source
material for a smaller language thins out quickly above B1, and a declared gap
is more useful than a padded one.

## Two things this course does not do

**It does not teach Nynorsk.** Norwegian has two official written standards.
A foreign-language course cannot carry both well, so this one commits to
Bokmål — the written form of roughly 85–90 % of Norwegians and the
best-resourced for speech synthesis — and treats Nynorsk as a recognition
skill: an awareness unit at B1 and an appendix, so that a reader meeting a
Nynorsk text knows what it is and can read it.

**It does not teach a single spoken standard as though one existed.** Norway
has no spoken standard. The audio commits to Eastern (Oslo) Bokmål because it
has to commit to something, says so plainly, and notes the major dialect
differences without asking A1–B1 learners to produce them. The two tonemes
(*bønder* / *bønner*) are described in the pronunciation stage and heard in the
audio; they are never a graded production requirement at these levels.

## The Scandinavian thread

Written Bokmål is largely readable to Danish and Swedish readers, and the
reverse holds too. That is a real asset for a learner, and the course makes it
explicit: a first neighbour-languages unit at A1, a receptive Danish and
Swedish reading unit at A2, and a mutual-intelligibility appendix that scopes
the claim properly — **receptive, not productive**. Reading a Swedish notice
after a year of Norwegian is realistic; speaking Danish is not, and the course
does not suggest it.

## Materials and audio

Slide decks and worksheets are generated locally from the branded LaTeX
templates in the boulingua *slidegen* and *sheetgen* projects, in this course's
signature colour, and are committed to the repository as `.odp` and PDF —
continuous integration only verifies them, it never builds them. Audio is
synthesised with an openly-licensed native Bokmål voice and shipped as
OGG/Opus alongside its transcript, so every recording is usable without sound.
Where the voice does not clear the *æ/ø/å* and minimal-pair check for a given
segment, that segment ships as transcript only and the gap is logged rather
than hidden.
