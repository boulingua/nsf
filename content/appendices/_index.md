## Why appendices rather than repetition

A course with three levels and thirty-eight units has a choice: restate the
gender rules in every unit that touches them, or state them once, properly,
and link. This course links. The consequence is that the appendices carry real
weight — the grammar reference is the place a learner is sent to at A1, A2 and
B1 alike, and it has to be written to serve all three without being useless to
any of them.

The V2 rule and its exceptions, subordinate-clause order, the *en/ei/et*

### Uttale og tonem — pronunciation guide
The fuller treatment behind the [Uttale stage](/uttale/): the vowel system,
vowel length, *kj/skj/sj/rs*, silent letters, and a descriptive account of the
two tonemes with minimal pairs.

### Nabospråk — mutual intelligibility
Bokmål against Danish and Swedish: the systematic spelling correspondences,
the *falske venner*, what transfers receptively and what does not. The page
that scopes the course's Scandinavian claim.
{{< /card >}}

{{< card >}}
{{< kicker >}}SCANDINAVIAN{{< /kicker >}}

### Nynorsk — an awareness note
What Nynorsk is, where it came from, how much of Norway writes it, and the
correspondences that let a Bokmål reader read it. Recognition, not production.
{{< /card >}}

{{< card >}}
{{< kicker >}}DIAGNOSTIC{{< /kicker >}}

### Typiske feil — common errors
Interference errors from German and from English, with the reason each one
happens: *ich habe gewesen* → *jeg har vært* / *jeg var*, article and gender
transfer, V2 lost after an adverbial, *å* dropped or doubled.
{{< /card >}}

### Vurderingskriterier — writing rubrics
Level-specific criteria for the written tasks and the model exams: task
achievement, range, accuracy, cohesion, and how the pass mark is arrived at.
{{< /card >}}

{{< card >}}
{{< kicker >}}FOR TEACHERS{{< /kicker >}}

### Teaching workflow
How to run a unit in a lesson: the five moves and their timing, where to
differentiate, what to do with the audio when the room has no sound, and how
the deck, the worksheet and the article are meant to divide the work.
